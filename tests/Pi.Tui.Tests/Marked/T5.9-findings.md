## Performance

Tokenizer methods and extension delegates take `SourceView`: the original string, an offset and a length. Slicing
is zero-copy, and bounded regex calls treat the view as the whole input, as JavaScript treats a sliced string.

Measured on 2026-09-16, Release build, back to back with marked on Node v24.18.0 on the same documents. Each figure
is the median of five runs after one warm-up. These are machine measurements, not timing assertions.

| Document | marked `plain` | Port `plain` | marked `pi` | Port `pi` | Port allocation, `plain` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Corpus, 100 KB | 24.6 ms | 92.6 ms | 30.0 ms | 73.8 ms | 9.6 MB |
| Corpus, 1 MB | 229.5 ms | 452.1 ms | 286.5 ms | 679.9 ms | 97.6 MB |
| One 200 KB paragraph | 854.0 ms | 75.4 ms | 1,140.9 ms | 323.9 ms | 18.9 MB |
| List, 100 KB | 26.7 ms | 50.9 ms | 32.9 ms | 40.6 ms | 17.3 MB |
| List, 300 KB | 88.0 ms | 182.7 ms | 96.4 ms | 111.1 ms | 50.2 MB |

- 1 MB took 4.9 times as long as 100 KB in `plain` and 9.2 times in `pi`, against the packet's 15-times ceiling.
  marked took 9.3 and 9.6 times.
- The port is 2 to 4 times slower than marked on the corpus documents, about twice as slow on `plain` lists, within
  a quarter on `pi` lists, and faster on the paragraph. At `ed89414` the 1 MB document took 5,121 ms and 4.5 GB in
  `plain`, and 26,679 ms and 35.9 GB in `pi`.
- `Lexer("a")` costs 0.066 ms and 34 KB (`plain`), and 0.096 ms and 66 KB (`pi`). At `ed89414` it cost 2.7 to
  6.7 ms and about 0.9 MB, because every lexer rebuilt its rule set.

## Nesting

marked recurses once per nesting level until V8 throws. Bisected on Node v24.18.0 with default stack size:

| Shape | `plain` | `pi` |
|---|---:|---:|
| Blockquotes, `> ` repeated | 2,000 | 2,000 |
| Lists, `- ` repeated | 2,875 | 2,000 |
| Ordered lists, `1. ` repeated | 2,000 | 2,360 |
| Emphasis, `*` and `_` alternating | 3,501 | 3,128 |
| Strong and emphasis alternating | 3,293 | 3,128 |

The failure is a `RangeError`; near the limit V8 can also throw a `SyntaxError` while compiling a regex. The limit
moves with JIT state.

The delivered findings reported that the port reached at least 10,000 levels. It did not: the probe caught the
exception and the test checked only that the process exited. On a thread with 1 MB of stack, the port lexed at most
291 levels of lists and 407 of blockquotes, then threw `InsufficientExecutionStackException`.

The port now:
- calls `RuntimeHelpers.TryEnsureSufficientExecutionStack()` on entry to `BlockTokens` and `InlineTokens`;
- continues on a new thread with 64 MB of stack when the stack is nearly full;
- throws `InsufficientExecutionStackException` beyond `Lexer.MaxNestingDepth`, 5,000 levels.

Measured on a thread with 1 MB of stack; a 256 KB stack gives the same outcomes:

| Shape, `plain` | 3,500 levels | 5,000 levels | 5,001 levels | 10,000 levels |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Blockquotes | tokens, 322 ms | tokens, 518 ms | exception, 421 ms | exception, 1,212 ms |
| Lists | tokens, 1,100 ms | tokens, 2,575 ms | exception, 2,853 ms | exception, 8,453 ms |
| Emphasis | tokens, 4,634 ms | tokens, 8,715 ms | exception, 9,147 ms | not run |

Nesting costs time in proportion to depth times remaining text, in marked as well: its 10,000-level list spends
2.4 s before its `RangeError`. Nested emphasis grew faster than that before this change. Reading scan captures
allocated a `Match` per delimiter, and every collection walked the deep stack: 3,000 levels allocated 6.8 GB and
spent 5.9 s in collections. The scans now enumerate matches without captures, and 3,000 levels allocate 212 MB.

Tests:
- `ten thousand block nesting levels do not crash the process` runs 10,000 levels of blockquotes and of lists in
  a child process and asserts the exception, not merely survival.
- `nesting lexes beyond a small thread stack up to the nesting limit` lexes 1,000 levels of blockquotes, lists
  and emphasis on a 256 KB stack, checks each token tree's depth, lexes 5,000 levels, and asserts the exception
  at 5,001.

## Deliberate scope and overrides

- `Renderer`, `TextRenderer`, `Parser`, `Hooks`, `marked.ts`, HTML rendering, the `Markdown` component,
  `trimPartialClosingFences`, `MemoryTerminal` SGR work, `mermaid.ts`, and the 81 renderer cases belong to
  T5.10 or later waves.
- The port supports the packet's `gfm: true`, `pedantic: false`, `breaks: false`, `silent: false`, and
  `async: false` path, plus tokenizer and block and inline token extensions. Unsupported flag values throw a
  `NotSupportedException` naming the option. Hooks, `walkTokens`, renderer extensions, `childTokens`, async
  lexing, `gfm: false`, pedantic mode, and breaks mode are not exposed.
- Extensions are validated when `Use` or `SetOptions` receives them, as marked's `use()` does.
- The pi LaTeX extension is ported as parser configuration. It works on `SourceView` and the generated `Pi*`
  patterns, and copies no remaining source.
- `Tokenizer` is public, non-sealed and virtual. `Tokenizer.Rules` returns the public `MarkedRules.Instance`,
  marked's `this.rules`, so subclasses outside `Pi.Tui` can reach it. `MarkdownParser` is internal, exposed to
  the test assembly through the packet's `InternalsVisibleTo` entry.
- `TokenJson`, the comparison writer, lives in the tests, as the packet placed it.
- The 5,000-level nesting limit is an override: marked has no limit of its own beyond V8's stack.
- No NuGet package, Markdig dependency, or Node build or test dependency was added, and nothing under
  `reference/` changed. The marked MIT licence is copied to `src/Pi.Tui/Marked/LICENSE` and referenced by the
  ported source headers.
